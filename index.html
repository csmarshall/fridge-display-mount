Three designs, one plate

One display, one fridge, three ways to hold the plate up. Design 3 is what is being ordered; the other two are its parents and each has its own page.

DESIGN 3 — BEING ORDERED: the hook, with the plate prepared for strutsopen

Design 1's plate, cut at 0.119 in with four extra O8.5 holes in two rows (17.7 and 220.9 mm above the bottom edge) at 187.28 centres. Phase 1 is the hook: arm over the top, 4 body magnets holding the plate flat. If that proves too lively, phase 2 bolts two 5 ft struts through those holes onto design 2's feet and lower clamp, and the magnets come off. Nothing about the plate changes between the phases.

First order $476.52; the support kit $207.48 only if needed. Touch flex at the screen edge: 0.057 mm on magnets, 0.118 mm on struts (strip model; the FEA sheet says 0.065 / 0.036). Generated by the hook generator, audited, in strut/dxf/.

Two low-profile slotted struts up the side panel, clamped top and bottom by a pair of identical L brackets, standing on the floor through an outboard foot. Decisions

settled Magnet layout and count — the four corners are provably optimal

Magnet layout and count — the four corners are provably optimalsettled

Exhaustive search, 1.25M layouts, under your margins (7.99 mm of plate beyond every disc = 12.7x the 0.63 mm tolerance stack; 6 mm between adjacent discs). Result: the FOUR CORNERS at inset 32 are the single best positions on the plate — 43.4 units of any-angle attachment per magnet, more than any other arrangement. Going 4 -> 8 body buys +97% attachment for +100% magnets: essentially linear, 1.7% LESS efficient per magnet. There is no knee. Symmetric counts are 4/8/12 only — the centre vent and the four vent windows occupy both centrelines, so no symmetric 6 exists on the body.

SETTLED: 4 body + 4 arm, McMaster 3506K67 (O48 x 11.5 mm, 175 lb rated, 61.2 lb derated on painted sheet). That reads 37x on the governing touch-torsion case and lets go at 146 lb — about 17x the display's own 8.7 lb. Magnet spend $191.36 at $23.92 each, qty 1, 2026-08-27. Count was always an insurance decision rather than an optimisation — the magnets carry NONE of the weight; the hook does. Spare holes are cut at the four mid-sides and on the arm, so going up in count later needs no recut.
settled What it takes to pull it off — by direction

What it takes to pull it off — by directionsettled

grab the BOTTOM edge and pull: 146 lb (the easiest place to grab, so the honest figure). grab the MIDDLE and pull: 245 lb (shorter lever than the bottom edge). grab the TOP edge and pull: 751 lb (pulling almost straight into the magnets).

Caveat: these assume a rigid plate with every magnet releasing at once. Peeling beats them one at a time — lifting one corner of the arm breaks a single magnet at ~61 lb using the arm as the lever. So these are resistance-to-accident numbers, NOT removal forces. Weakest direction is sliding it along the panel, which MOVES it rather than detaching it — the hook does not resist that axis.
settled Vent windows: keep them

Vent windows: keep themsettled

Quoted live. Removing all four makes the part $0.68 DEARER ($97.38 vs $96.70) on an identical blank. 23% less cutting does not pay for the 148 cm² of steel you then keep — their material component tracks part area and cut time is cheap at this size.

No decision needed unless you want them gone for looks. They also save 116 g.
settled Lightening the plate to save material — not worth it

Lightening the plate to save material — not worth itsettled

Three live price points solve for the pricing model: about $4.00 per 100 cm2 of steel, $0.136 per inch of cut, and $16.22 fixed. So the $96.70 part is roughly $58 steel + $23 cutting + $16 setup. The consequence is a hard break-even: a cutout only pays for itself above Ø53 mm. Anything smaller costs more in cut time than the steel it saves — so a field of small lightening holes actively LOSES money. Removing a realistic 25% of the plate as Ø100 mm cutouts saves about $6.74, 7% of the part.

Recommend: no. ~$7 for real topology work and fresh recut risk, and it fights your own preference for the bracket being heavier rather than lighter. Caveat: the model is an exact fit on three points, so treat it as indicative — I can quote a deliberately lightened variant to validate it if you want the number nailed down.

settled Plate thickness — SETTLED at 0.187 in / 4.75 mm HRPO (SendCutSend list it as .188)

Plate thickness — SETTLED at 0.187 in / 4.75 mm HRPO (SendCutSend list it as .188)settled

Chosen for heft and margin, NOT for stiffness you can feel: plate flex under a touch is 0.016 mm here against 0.064 mm at 0.119 in, and neither is perceptible. What it buys is 7.37 kg instead of 3.71 kg, plus the best $/kg and stiffness-per-dollar in the material sweep. Hot-rolled is cheaper stock than cold-rolled, which is why .188 HRPO undercuts .135 CRS despite being thicker.

+$11.21 over the 0.119 build. Commercial TV mounts run 1.8-2.7 mm; this is 4.75 mm.
settled Portrait — on practical grounds, CORRECTED from "impossible"

Portrait — on practical grounds, CORRECTED from "impossible"settled

At the old 246 mm strut spacing landscape overhung the cabinet's REAR edge and was geometrically impossible. Narrowing the struts to 160 moved them forward, and landscape now technically fits — by 1.5 mm at the rear against 52.9 at the front.

That is flush with the back of the cabinet and wildly off centre, so portrait remains the choice. But the honest reason is now practical, not geometric, and the earlier claim on this page overstated it.
settled 5 ft struts and two bolt rows — SETTLED 2026-09-01

5 ft struts and two bolt rows — SETTLED 2026-09-01settled

A 4 ft strut put ONE slot row 17.7 mm above the plate edge and the plate cantilevered 144 mm to the VESA: 0.876 mm of screen-edge movement under a 5 lb press, four times the feel-rigid band and WORSE than the magnets. 5 ft puts 6 slot rows inside the plate; the lowest and highest that clear every magnet face, window and hole are 17.73 and 220.93, bracketing the VESA, and the plate becomes a beam between them: 0.118 mm by the strip model, 0.036 by FEA.

The rows are PICKED from the hook generator's own feature map and the generator re-validates them; if the mounting height moves they are re-picked and the build refuses if they no longer bracket. The strut stands 38 mm above the plate top, behind the display.
settled 4 body magnets in phase 1, arm magnets not bought — SETTLED 2026-09-01

4 body magnets in phase 1, arm magnets not bought — SETTLED 2026-09-01settled

The magnets are NOT optional in phase 1: nothing else holds the bottom of the plate to the panel. An earlier costing called them optional and was wrong. The four ARM magnets are anti-walk insurance with zero load credit; their holes are cut, they are bought only if the arm is seen to creep on the foam.

$95.68 at $23.92 each. They come OFF when the struts go on: the plate then sits 6.02 mm off the panel against the magnets' 11.51.
settled Plate thickness 0.119 in for design 3 — SETTLED, checked both ways

Plate thickness 0.119 in for design 3 — SETTLED, checked both wayssettled

Design 1 chose 0.187 in for heft, not stiffness — its own record says flex is imperceptible at either gauge. 0.119 in makes the whole kit one gauge (plate, clamp, feet share a bend spec) and hangs 2.1 kg less on the fridge top. Checked: neck SF 32x / body SF 34x on magnets, 41x / 36x on struts; the FEA plate model agrees with the strip model to ~15%.

Bend deduction is SendCutSend's published 4.97 mm for this gauge, one home for all three designs.

Powder coat at SendCutSend, or spray it yourselfopen

Matte black powder adds $66–70 and pushes delivery Aug 31 → Sep 3. Bare CRS will surface-rust in a kitchen, so it needs something.

Recommend: let them do it. A hand-sprayed edge on bare steel is where rust starts.

What gates the first order: the plate, the magnets, the bolts.

Clear window on the fridge top, front to back — MEASURED, TWICEopen

Two readings of the same cover: 2026-08-27 gave 406.4 mm clear from the rear edge (cover 203 deep); 2026-08-31 gave 400.05 (cover 209.55). Charles's word for the second was 'roughly'. The hook designs (1 and 3) centre the plate on the case depth, which puts the 190 mm arm's front edge 6.6 mm from the cover on the first reading and TOUCHING it on the second. The clamp design's top bar reaches only tens of mm and is not affected.

The hook generator still carries the first reading. Resolve with one measurement; if 400 stands, move the hook plate ~10 mm rearward (plate_from_rear_override) or lift the cover, which Charles says is removable.

Photograph the display's rear boxopen

TWO features in the box face, both SCALED off Waveshare's raster drawing to ±5 mm: the Pi fan at ~R82 (~30 dia) and the GPIO slot at ~R107. The hook plate's four vent windows sit at R87.5, an average of the two, so one covers the fan in every rotation; the clamp design's plate stops short of both.

Neither feature is dimensioned anywhere. One photo with a rule across the box settles the windows for designs 1 and 3 and the plate height for design 2.
