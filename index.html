Two designs

This project changed load path once. Both are here, and the difference is not a detail.

CURRENT — a clamped strut standing on the flooropen

Two 6 ft low-profile slotted struts up the side panel, clamped top and bottom by a pair of identical L brackets, standing on the floor through an outboard foot. The clamps hold it in; the floor carries the weight. The display height becomes adjustable after the fact, nothing depends on the fridge top's geometry, and there are NO MAGNETS — which removes the derate chain, the fastener stack, the peel failure mode and the dependency on the panel being magnetic at all.

Work continues in csmarshall/fridge-strut-mount. Two questions remain and both need a torch under the appliance: whether the lower clamp's reach fouls anything, and whether there is a rib worth hooking rather than bearing on. See the clamped-strut sheet under Diagrams.
settled PREVIOUS — a hook over the top, held flat by magnets

PREVIOUS — a hook over the top, held flat by magnetssettled

One bent plate: an arm reaching over the fridge top carrying the entire load into bearing at the corner, a neck down the side, and 8 magnets holding the plate flat. It is FINISHED — validated, audited 15/15, and quoted at $197.07. It is superseded for adjustability and floor loading, NOT for being wrong.

Everything below this section documents that design. Tagged hook-final in this repo if it is ever needed. Its magnet primer is worth reading whichever design wins — the physics of why magnet ratings mislead does not change.

Open decisions

Tagged by which design they belong to. [MAGNET HOOK] items are kept for the record and are NOT live — the clamped strut does not have magnets, an arm, or a plate thickness to choose.

settled [MAGNET HOOK] Magnet layout and count — the four corners are provably optimal

[MAGNET HOOK] Magnet layout and count — the four corners are provably optimalsettled

Exhaustive search, 1.25M layouts, under your margins (7.99 mm of plate beyond every disc = 12.7x the 0.63 mm tolerance stack; 6 mm between adjacent discs). Result: the FOUR CORNERS at inset 32 are the single best positions on the plate — 43.4 units of any-angle attachment per magnet, more than any other arrangement. Going 4 -> 8 body buys +97% attachment for +100% magnets: essentially linear, 1.7% LESS efficient per magnet. There is no knee. Symmetric counts are 4/8/12 only — the centre vent and the four vent windows occupy both centrelines, so no symmetric 6 exists on the body.

SETTLED: 4 body + 4 arm, McMaster 3506K67 (O48 x 11.5 mm, 175 lb rated, 61.2 lb derated on painted sheet). That reads 37x on the governing touch-torsion case and lets go at 146 lb — about 17x the display's own 8.7 lb. Magnet spend $191.36 at $23.92 each, qty 1, 2026-08-27. Count was always an insurance decision rather than an optimisation — the magnets carry NONE of the weight; the hook does. Spare holes are cut at the four mid-sides and on the arm, so going up in count later needs no recut.
settled [MAGNET HOOK] What it takes to pull it off — by direction

[MAGNET HOOK] What it takes to pull it off — by directionsettled

grab the BOTTOM edge and pull: 146 lb (the easiest place to grab, so the honest figure). grab the MIDDLE and pull: 245 lb (shorter lever than the bottom edge). grab the TOP edge and pull: 751 lb (pulling almost straight into the magnets).

Caveat: these assume a rigid plate with every magnet releasing at once. Peeling beats them one at a time — lifting one corner of the arm breaks a single magnet at ~61 lb using the arm as the lever. So these are resistance-to-accident numbers, NOT removal forces. Weakest direction is sliding it along the panel, which MOVES it rather than detaching it — the hook does not resist that axis.
settled [MAGNET HOOK] Vent windows: keep them

[MAGNET HOOK] Vent windows: keep themsettled

Quoted live. Removing all four makes the part $0.68 DEARER ($97.38 vs $96.70) on an identical blank. 23% less cutting does not pay for the 148 cm² of steel you then keep — their material component tracks part area and cut time is cheap at this size.

No decision needed unless you want them gone for looks. They also save 116 g.
settled [MAGNET HOOK] Lightening the plate to save material — not worth it

[MAGNET HOOK] Lightening the plate to save material — not worth itsettled

Three live price points solve for the pricing model: about $4.00 per 100 cm2 of steel, $0.136 per inch of cut, and $16.22 fixed. So the $96.70 part is roughly $58 steel + $23 cutting + $16 setup. The consequence is a hard break-even: a cutout only pays for itself above Ø53 mm. Anything smaller costs more in cut time than the steel it saves — so a field of small lightening holes actively LOSES money. Removing a realistic 25% of the plate as Ø100 mm cutouts saves about $6.74, 7% of the part.

Recommend: no. ~$7 for real topology work and fresh recut risk, and it fights your own preference for the bracket being heavier rather than lighter. Caveat: the model is an exact fit on three points, so treat it as indicative — I can quote a deliberately lightened variant to validate it if you want the number nailed down.
settled [MAGNET HOOK] How far the arm reaches onto the fridge top — SETTLED at 180 mm

[MAGNET HOOK] How far the arm reaches onto the fridge top — SETTLED at 180 mmsettled

Reach costs sheet one-for-one, and the cost is linear: about $16.50 per 100 mm powder-coated — $8.68 laser and material, $7.80 coating, with bending a flat $11.77 that does not scale. Coated: 130 mm $174.30 / 230 mm $190.78 / 330 mm $207.26 / 430 mm $223.74 / 530 mm $240.22. Slopes are fitted from the two LIVE quotes at 130 and 180 mm, so the far end is projected rather than verified.

Neither the pad budget nor the 1118 mm sheet limit binds before 530 mm — with the top measured flat, reach costs sheet but no pad. What binds is FIT: hinge covers stand 36.5 mm proud, and the cabinet is only 609.6 mm deep, so 330 mm is already more than halfway across the top. Decide on the measured clear window, not price.
settled [BOTH] Screen centre at 1331 mm — APPROVED 2026-08-27

[BOTH] Screen centre at 1331 mm — APPROVED 2026-08-27settled

A 5 ft 1 in viewer's eye line (1448 mm) lands on the screen. A 6 ft 4 in viewer's (1805 mm) sits 196 mm ABOVE the top edge, so they look down at it. Normal for a fridge-side display, but it is a choice.

Charles confirmed the viewing height looks right on the actual fridge. Adjustable with --screen-centre-height if that ever changes.
settled [MAGNET HOOK] Plate thickness — SETTLED at 0.187 in / 4.75 mm HRPO (SendCutSend list it as .188)

[MAGNET HOOK] Plate thickness — SETTLED at 0.187 in / 4.75 mm HRPO (SendCutSend list it as .188)settled

Chosen for heft and margin, NOT for stiffness you can feel: plate flex under a touch is 0.016 mm here against 0.064 mm at 0.119 in, and neither is perceptible. What it buys is 7.37 kg instead of 3.71 kg, plus the best $/kg and stiffness-per-dollar in the material sweep. Hot-rolled is cheaper stock than cold-rolled, which is why .188 HRPO undercuts .135 CRS despite being thicker.

+$11.21 over the 0.119 build. Commercial TV mounts run 1.8-2.7 mm; this is 4.75 mm.

[BOTH] Powder coat at SendCutSend, or spray it yourselfopen

Matte black powder adds $66–70 and pushes delivery Aug 31 → Sep 3. Bare CRS will surface-rust in a kitchen, so it needs something.

Recommend: let them do it. A hand-sprayed edge on bare steel is where rust starts.

Before ordering

Measurements that gate an order. [MAGNET HOOK] ones no longer gate anything: the clamp design does not care whether the panel is magnetic or what the top corner radius is. That is the point of it.

settled [BOTH] Clear window on the fridge top, front to back — MEASURED 2026-08-27

[BOTH] Clear window on the fridge top, front to back — MEASURED 2026-08-27settled

406 mm clear from the rear edge to the hinge cover, against a 190 mm arm width — fits with 216 mm to spare (2.14x). The hinge cover occupies the front 203 mm and is removable/adjustable, so even a clash would be recoverable. This was the likeliest recut risk; it is now closed.

[BOTH] Height to the TOP OF THE CASE, not the hingesblocked

Design assumes Samsung's published 1743 mm. Sets the neck length and therefore the screen height.

settled [MAGNET HOOK] Is the side panel actually magnetic? — YES, measured 2026-08-26

[MAGNET HOOK] Is the side panel actually magnetic? — YES, measured 2026-08-26settled

Checked on the actual unit: the TOP and the SIDES are both magnetic. That confirms the arm retention magnets will do their job, so all eight are fitted in the first order. It also demotes the non-magnetic-panel fallback behind the 190 mm arm width from a design driver to free insurance.

The hook carries all the weight either way — this was never load-bearing.

[MAGNET HOOK] Top corner radiusblocked

The 11.5 mm pad covers up to R35 mm, so this is a confirmation rather than an input.

[BOTH] Photograph the display's rear boxopen

The Pi fan opening is at R87.5 mm from the VESA centre, scaled off a raster drawing to ±5 mm. The vent windows sit on that radius.

Only matters once the panel is in hand.

[BOTH] VESA insert thread depthopen

Sets the M4 screw length. Any standard head clears the fridge inside the 12 mm standoff.
