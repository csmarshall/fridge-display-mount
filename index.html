Open decisions

Things waiting on you. Everything else is settled.

Magnet layout and count — the four corners are provably optimalsettled

Exhaustive search, 1.25M layouts, under your margins (12 mm of plate beyond every disc = 19x the 0.63 mm tolerance stack; 6 mm between adjacent discs). Result: the FOUR CORNERS at inset 30 are the single best positions on the plate — 43.4 units of any-angle attachment per magnet, more than any other arrangement. Going 4 -> 8 body buys +97% attachment for +100% magnets: essentially linear, 1.7% LESS efficient per magnet. There is no knee. Symmetric counts are 4/8/12 only — the centre vent and the four vent windows occupy both centrelines, so no symmetric 6 exists on the body.

SETTLED: 4 body + 6 arm, McMaster 3506K67 (O48 x 11.5 mm, 175 lbf rated, 61.2 lbf derated on painted sheet). That reads 37x on the governing touch-torsion case and lets go at 194 lbf — about 22x the display's own 8.7 lbf. Magnet spend $239.20 at $23.92 each, qty 1, 2026-08-25. Count was always an insurance decision rather than an optimisation — the magnets carry NONE of the weight; the hook does. Spare holes are cut at the four mid-sides and on the arm, so going up in count later needs no recut.

What it takes to pull it off — by directionsettled

Pull straight out, gripping the BOTTOM edge: 194 lbf (the worst place to grab — longest lever). Pull straight out, gripping the MIDDLE: 325 lbf (where a hand naturally lands). Pull straight out, gripping the TOP edge: 997 lbf (the display would fail first). Lift the whole thing straight UP: 433 lbf (this is how you would try to remove it — see note). Slide it along the panel, front to back: 122 lbf (MOVES it rather than detaching it — the hook does not resist this direction). Press the screen edge until it twists off: 186 lbf (a firm touch press is about 5 lbf).

Caveat: these assume a rigid plate with every magnet releasing at once. Peeling beats them one at a time — lifting one corner of the arm breaks a single magnet at ~61 lbf using the arm as the lever. So these are resistance-to-accident numbers, NOT removal forces. Weakest direction is sliding it along the panel, which MOVES it rather than detaching it — the hook does not resist that axis.

Vent windows: keep themsettled

Quoted live. Removing all four makes the part $0.68 DEARER ($97.38 vs $96.70) on an identical blank. 23% less cutting does not pay for the 148 cm² of steel you then keep — their material component tracks part area and cut time is cheap at this size.

No decision needed unless you want them gone for looks. They also save 116 g.

Lightening the plate to save material — not worth itsettled

Three live price points solve for the pricing model: about $4.00 per 100 cm2 of steel, $0.136 per inch of cut, and $16.22 fixed. So the $96.70 part is roughly $58 steel + $23 cutting + $16 setup. The consequence is a hard break-even: a cutout only pays for itself above Ø53 mm. Anything smaller costs more in cut time than the steel it saves — so a field of small lightening holes actively LOSES money. Removing a realistic 25% of the plate as Ø100 mm cutouts saves about $6.74, 7% of the part.

Recommend: no. ~$7 for real topology work and fresh recut risk, and it fights your own preference for the bracket being heavier rather than lighter. Caveat: the model is an exact fit on three points, so treat it as indicative — I can quote a deliberately lightened variant to validate it if you want the number nailed down.

How far the arm reaches onto the fridge topblocked

Reach costs sheet one-for-one, and the cost is linear: about $16.50 per 100 mm powder-coated — $8.68 laser and material, $7.80 coating, with bending a flat $11.77 that does not scale. Coated: 130 mm $174.30 / 230 mm $190.78 / 330 mm $207.26 / 430 mm $223.74 / 530 mm $240.22. Slopes are fitted from the two LIVE quotes at 130 and 180 mm, so the far end is projected rather than verified.

Neither the pad budget nor the 1118 mm sheet limit binds before 530 mm — the crown requirement plateaus once the arm is near the crest. What binds is FIT: hinge covers stand 36.5 mm proud, and the cabinet is only 609.6 mm deep, so 330 mm is already more than halfway across the top. Decide on the measured clear window, not price.

Before ordering

Four measurements gate the order. Nothing else does.

Clear window on the fridge top, front to back — MEASURED 2026-08-27settled

406 mm clear from the rear edge to the hinge cover, against a 190 mm arm width — fits with 216 mm to spare (2.14x). The hinge cover occupies the front 203 mm and is removable/adjustable, so even a clash would be recoverable. This was the likeliest recut risk; it is now closed.

Height to the TOP OF THE CASE, not the hingesblocked

Design assumes Samsung's published 1743 mm. Sets the neck length and therefore the screen height.

Is the side panel actually magnetic? — YES, measured 2026-08-26settled

Checked on the actual unit: the TOP and the SIDES are both magnetic. That confirms the arm retention magnets will do their job, so all six are fitted in the first order. It also demotes the non-magnetic-panel fallback behind the 190 mm arm width from a design driver to free insurance.

The hook carries all the weight either way — this was never load-bearing.

Top corner radiusblocked

The 11.5 mm pad covers up to R34 mm, so this is a confirmation rather than an input.

Photograph the display's rear boxopen

The Pi fan opening is at R87.5 mm from the VESA centre, scaled off a raster drawing to ±5 mm. The vent windows sit on that radius.

Only matters once the panel is in hand.

VESA insert thread depthopen

Sets the M4 screw length. Any standard head clears the fridge inside the 12 mm standoff.
