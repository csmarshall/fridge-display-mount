## Why this and not the others

- **Design 1 (0.187 in):** $10 more, 2.1 kg heavier, and it has no strut holes so the fallback is
  a recut. Its stiffness advantage (0.022 vs 0.085 mm at the screen edge under a 5 lb press) is
  below anything a finger notices. Nothing else differs. `gauge_magnet_matrix.svg`.
- **Design 2 (struts + clamp):** more parts, floor pads, a clamp under the fridge, and a plate
  that shares nothing with the hook. It is the answer only if the hook fails in use, and design 3
  already carries that answer in four holes. `clamp.html`.
- **Design 4 (stock aluminium):** rejected 2026-09-02, hand drilling.
- **Aluminium plate at SendCutSend:** cheaper on the 2026-08-25 cut-only sweep, but flex is 3x
  steel's at the same gauge, no live quote on the real file, and no reason to reopen the material
  when the steel plate is $187.
- **OSH Cut instead of SendCutSend:** $204.26 coated, $17 more. Their standby tier at $60.93 is
  bent and bare, needs the flat regenerated for their bend table, and you keep the coat. Not
  worth a second vendor flow. `vendors.svg`.
- **Other magnets:** 8 x MM-C-32 is the cheapest set to 6x on a 20 lb grab using the eight
  positions the plate has, on every gauge. Bigger magnets only add cost; smaller ones need four
  holes that are not cut. `magnet_economics.svg`, `gauge_magnet_matrix.svg`.

**The gauge question (`gauge_magnet_matrix.svg`, live sweep of every bendable gauge on the real file):**
the thickest steel with a published bend spec is 0.187 in HRPO at $199.90, and hot-rolled makes it
CHEAPER than 0.135 in CRS ($235.83). It is 4x stiffer than 0.119 in (0.022 vs 0.085 mm at the screen
edge) for $12.70 and 2.1 kg. Aluminium at 0.100 or 0.125 in is the cheapest bendable, coatable plate
($159) but flexes 2.5-5x more than 0.119 in steel; aluminium at 0.187 in costs $233. The plate with
strut holes was regenerated at 0.187 in (`strut/dxf/H_hook_plate_187.dxf`, audited 15/15, neck 78x,
body 56x). **Recommendation: 0.187 in HRPO with the strut holes, $199.90.** The one cost is the nut
stack: a 10 mm stud with a 4.75 mm plate, 1.7 mm washer and a ~4 mm jam nut is at the limit, and the
K&J drawing gives the stud as 10 +/-2.5 mm. Order the magnets first; if the stud measures short, drop
the washer or use a 3 mm M6 jam nut. Nothing cut changes.

**The magnet band (`removability.svg`):** against estimated human forces (bump 20 lb, one hand 45 lb,
two hands 100 lb), the band is "slide resistance at least 1.5x a bump, lift-off within two hands".
4 x MM-C-32 is under the bump margin (21 lb). 6 x is in the band (slide 32 lb, lift 50 lb). 8 x is in
the band (slide 42 lb, lift 61 lb, 65 lb on the heavier plate). **Recommendation: buy 8, fit 8.** The
35 % derate is the largest unknown and it flatters the magnets; 8 keeps margin against it. If the
mount fights you when repositioning, take two out — a two-minute change, no parts to buy.

**Stud length is now verified:** K&J drawing MM-C-32.pdf, overall 18 mm, body 8 mm, stud 10 +/-2.5 mm.
